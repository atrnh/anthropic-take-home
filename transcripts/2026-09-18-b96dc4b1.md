# Theory: functional quality vs deep quality

Paraphrased from Daniele Procida, "Towards a theory of quality in documentation", Diátaxis
(https://diataxis.fr/quality/). Read that page for the original argument.

## Why distinguish them

We rarely define "quality". Still, people can point to excellent documentation and tend to
agree when they see lapses. That shared intuition is worth sharpening. Splitting quality into
two kinds makes it something you can discuss and act on.

## Functional quality

Accuracy, completeness, consistency, usefulness, precision, and similar standards. Missing any
one of them means a key function is failing.

- The dimensions are **independent**. Writing can be accurate but incomplete. It can be
  complete but inaccurate and inconsistent. It can be all of these and still useless.
- They are **objective** and sometimes literally countable (completeness).
- Meeting them takes discipline, attention to detail, and technical skill. Any failure is
  **obvious to the reader**.

## Deep quality

Feeling good to use, having flow, fitting human needs, being beautiful, anticipating the user.

- You **can't check or measure** these, but you can clearly identify them. Most people
  recognise them when they meet them.
- Writing can pass every functional test and still be awkward and unpleasant. Accurate,
  consistent, even useful material can lack deep quality.

## Claude

## Verdict

The individual procedures are mostly sound, but the page has functional gaps in the part that sends readers to a guide: the most common organizational reader, a Team or Enterprise member, isn't told which guide to use. The two variants also differ in wording where the product probably doesn't differ. Deep quality is held back by one main thing: **the routing text is written for the uncertain minority but placed in front of everyone.** A second problem is that the variants were never harmonized, which undermines the "Show all guides" comparison the page itself offers.

(`verify.py` passes and the build is current, so all findings are about the content.)

## Reader and need

The reader is a Claude Desktop user who wants a plugin working. They are usually task-focused and not anxious. Some have been handed a repo or a `.zip` by a colleague. Some don't know how their account is provisioned. The page is a **how-to guide**, with a small routing step placed in front of it. The modes are mostly clean. The only mixing is that the routing section reads like an explanation, and it sits between the reader and the steps.

## Functional quality

**Accuracy**
- **page.md:7–8:** "depend on how your organization provides Claude". The next section's first case is an individual, who has no organization. Fix: "depend on how you get access to Claude."
- **page.md:3:** The page says plugins extend "Cowork and Code", but both guides are for Claude Desktop only. A Claude Code CLI user arrives through that sentence and finds no procedure for them. Fix: scope the intro to Claude Desktop and link to the Claude Code plugin docs. *Unverified:* whether Desktop's Code tab shares these plugins.
- **cowork.md:31:** The heading "Git marketplace **or URL**" suggests the method also covers a direct plugin URL, but it only covers a repository URL. Fix: "Install from a Git repository".
- **cowork.md:22–29, 37–48, 56–61:** **Discover**, **Add**, **Yours**, **Sync**, and **Upload a plugin** are *unverified*. The snapshot says **Browse plugins** / **Install**, and the changelog already flags this. I'm only noting that nothing on the page marks it.

**Completeness**
- **page.md:14–19:** No instruction covers the Team/Enterprise member who isn't on Government. That reader matches neither "individual" nor "Claude for Government". The "not sure" sentence reads as applying only to the Government question. Fix: "If you use Claude on your own or through a Team or Enterprise plan, use General." Confirm that claim first. The General guide's "Provision plugins for an organization" link suggests it holds.
- **cowork.md:8–16 vs 56–59:** The callout says to review permissions and data access *before* installing. The file method uploads and installs in one step, and inspection only comes afterwards (step 4). Either say how to review a package before uploading it, or scope the callout to the marketplace methods.
- **cowork.md:50–61:** There is no connector sign-in step, although both other methods have one. The package format isn't stated either (Government says `.zip`).
- **cowork.md:31–48:** Private repositories aren't covered. A team sharing a private GitHub repo is the typical case, and the source calls this "the typical way teams distribute". What happens at **Sync**?
- **cowork.md (overall):** Required plugins on Team/Enterprise aren't mentioned. The Government guide tells its reader that auto-installed plugins are already there, but the General guide doesn't. One sentence would cover it. The changelog records this omission but doesn't justify it.

## Deep quality

Read as a General user, the page makes you wade through about ten lines of routing, including a blockquote script for emailing an administrator. Only then do you reach a chooser that has already selected your guide. The steps themselves then move well until the marketplace method. There, step 2 is something to look at rather than do, step 3 and step 5 both say "inspect its components", and the success signal comes after an optional configuration step. The Git method's "This **should** take you to…" passes the author's evidentiary doubt on to the reader just when they want confirmation.

Read as a Government user, the flow is better: restrictions come first, "already installed" is anticipated, and the device-local scope is stated. But if they choose **Show all guides** to confirm they're in the right place, they see a dozen differences. They can't tell which ones are real product differences and which are just wording.

**Root causes**
1. **The routing is arranged around the author's caution, not the reader's likelihood.** The help exists because the page can't classify accounts, and that honesty is right. But it is placed as if every reader were uncertain.
2. **The variants were transcribed from their sources rather than written as a pair.** A comparison feature raises the bar: every difference that isn't a product difference is noise the reader has to rule out.

**Inventive fixes**
- **Put the chooser first and make the help a disclosure:** "Not sure which guide? ▸" next to the select control. The default reader goes straight to the steps. The uncertain reader still gets the admin script, and it still sits beside the chooser (SCOPE-02).
- **Harmonize the variants against each other:** write one shared skeleton (applicability → callout → method intro → steps → result) and identical phrasing wherever the product behaves the same. Remaining differences then signal real ones. You could even add one sentence at the top of Government: "Differences from the general guide: no public marketplace, no plugin connectors, no local MCP servers."
- **Make the security callout usable at the moment it matters.** In marketplace step 3, say what to look for, for example: "Connectors reach external services; hooks run scripts on your computer." Then "review before installing" becomes an action the reader can take.
- **Tighten the marketplace method to four steps:** merge step 2 into step 3, put "listed under **Yours**" directly after **Add**, and move enable/disable to a closing line such as "To turn off a component, open the plugin from **Yours**."
- **Replace "This should take you to…"** with a stated observable result, and keep the uncertainty in the changelog.
- **Anticipate the wrong-guide moment.** If a Government reader's UI shows **Discover**, or a General reader's shows **Organization plugins**, that's a strong sign they're in the wrong guide. Mention it once in the help.

## What already works

- **Every method opens with "Use this method when…".** The reader chooses a method from their situation (someone shared a repo, someone shared a file), not from the mechanism. Keep this.
- **The admin question in a blockquote** gives an uncertain reader exact words to send, which turns a dead end into a next action.
- **Each method is self-contained from Customize → Plugins** and ends with an observable result that is carefully kept separate from connector access.
- **Government restrictions come before the steps, and its result repeats the connector consequence.** This is the right redundancy at the right place.
- **The package limits link sits right before the upload**, and the `owner/repo` shorthand is offered where it's useful.
- **Next steps stay as links**, so the page keeps one outcome.

## Revisions, in priority order

1. Fix the routing gap: name the Team/Enterprise path, correct "your organization" for individuals, and scope the intro to Claude Desktop with a link for Claude Code CLI users.
2. Resolve the contradiction between the security callout and the file method, and rename "Git marketplace or URL".
3. Fill the small completeness holes: file-method sign-in and format, private repos, required plugins in General, a pointer to the Government marketplace, and an MCP link.
4. Standardize the naming: product (Desktop/Cowork) and catalog (catalog/directory/marketplace).
5. Move the help into a disclosure next to the chooser.
6. Harmonize the two variants to one skeleton and one set of phrasing, and give the callouts distinct titles.
7. Local flow fixes: tighten the marketplace steps, remove "should", fix "Check" and "the plugin", and make the security callout actionable in step 3.
